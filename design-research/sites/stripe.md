# Stripe (craft benchmark)

- **URL:** https://stripe.com (200, canonical)
- **Title:** "Stripe | Financial Infrastructure to Grow Your Revenue"
- **Meta description:** "Stripe is a financial services platform that helps all types of businesses accept payments, build flexible billing models, and manage money movement."
- **Studied for:** trust architecture, type scale, and section craft. Not a healthcare reference.

## Layout system
Wide, confident container with a strong 12-column feel. Section rhythm alternates deliberately between full-bleed statements and contained multi-column blocks, which is why a very long page never feels monotonous. This is the **section-layout-variation** discipline executed better than anyone: no two consecutive sections share a layout family.

## Hero pattern
Headline "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue" with the supporting sentence "Accept payments, offer financial services, and implement custom revenue models, from your first transaction to your billionth." The "first transaction to your billionth" construction is the whole value proposition compressed into a range, and it is the best single sentence in any of the ten sites studied.

## Section flow (top to bottom)
1. Hero: "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue"
2. "Flexible solutions for every business model" with capability blocks: Accept and optimize payments globally / Enable any billing model / Monetize through agentic commerce / Create a card issuing program / Access borderless money movement / Embed payments in your platform
3. "The backbone of global commerce"
4. **"Powering businesses of all sizes"** with named customer stories: Hertz, URBN ("consolidates $5 billion in online and in-store revenue"), Instacart, Le Monde
5. Segment blocks: "Build a foundation for your startup" / "Make your SaaS platform a complete financial operating system"
6. "Reliable, extensible infrastructure for every stack" with "Connect to existing systems" / "Scale with confidence" / "Choose an integration path"
7. **"What's happening"** with hard numbers: "Businesses on Stripe generated $1.9T in 2025", "150K+ users have their best day ever on Stripe"

## Typography
Self-hosted (hash-named woff2). Sohne-family sans. The scale is the lesson rather than the family: Stripe's display sizes are large and confidently tracked-in, body copy sits at a comfortable reading measure, and the step between levels is always obvious. There is never ambiguity about which of two elements is more important.

## Color

|---|---|
| `#181818` / `#1F1F1F` / `#2D2D2D` | Near-black ramp |
| `#010202` | Deepest ink |
| `#002253` | Deep navy |
| `#FF6201` / `#FF543E` | Orange and coral accents |
| `#FC47AD` | Pink |
| `#FECC38` / `#FBBC05` / `#FBA32A` | Amber scale |
| `#4285F4`, `#EA4335`, `#34A853` | (Third-party brand marks, not Stripe's own palette) |

The pattern to note: a disciplined near-black ramp plus a **spectrum of saturated accents used as identity**, not as decoration. Stripe can carry many accents because the neutral structure underneath is so rigid.

## Components
Named customer story cards with real companies and real figures. Capability blocks. Integration path selector. A live-metrics band ("What's happening"). Logo treatment throughout uses real marks, never text wordmarks.

## Motion and interaction
Motion is present but subordinate. Gradient animation in hero areas, smooth scroll reveals, and hover states on cards. Nothing hijacks the scroll. Character: fluid and continuous rather than snappy.

## Imagery
Real product surfaces, real customer logos, high-fidelity UI. No stock photography, no illustration filler.

## Conversion mechanics
This is the strongest **trust architecture** of the ten sites, and the most transferable idea to a healthcare practice:

1. Named entities with real figures ("URBN consolidates $5 billion", "$1.9T in 2025")
2. Specificity over adjectives: never "trusted by many", always a number and a name
3. Segment-specific paths so each visitor sees themselves
4. Liveness signals ("What's happening", "best day ever")

## Tone of voice
Precise, confident, quantified. Every claim carries a number or a name. No filler verbs, no hedging.

## Steal for Newpoint
- **Every trust claim carries a number or a name.** Newpoint cannot claim scale, but it can be specific: two doctorate-prepared providers, two states, named insurers, a named evaluation length.
- Section-layout variation so no two consecutive sections share a family
- Type scale with unambiguous steps between levels
- Real logos for insurers rather than text wordmarks
- Range constructions that compress a value proposition ("from your first transaction to your billionth")

## Do not transplant
- Page length and section count
- Accent spectrum: Stripe can carry six accents because it is a platform brand. A two-provider clinical practice needs one.
- Enterprise segmentation architecture
